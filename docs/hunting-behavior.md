# Brown-bear hunt behavior used in Bear Rush

Brown bears are opportunistic omnivores, not pursuit specialists. A review of ungulate predation reports substantially more kills of newborn and yearling animals than adults. It describes scent-led searching, occasional chases, and bears as relatively poorly adapted to prolonged cursorial pursuit. It also records that natural hunts can last much longer than a practical game encounter. [1] [2]

For the actual takedown, the sources describe bites to the head/neck or back of the neck, and forepaw strikes that can injure the spine. Bears then feed at the carcass; they do not simply collect prey by brushing past it. [1] [3]

The game therefore uses a readable, compressed encounter: discover a track, approach quietly and upwind, trigger a flight response, keep a close moving pursuit, then use a short sprint and a committed close pounce when prey is visibly winded. A successful pounce plays a brief forelimb/head-down capture animation and awards the prey; a body collision or a pounce from too far away does not. The window and speeds are tuned for playability, not presented as a literal biological simulation. Fawn remains the most catchable target; larger ungulates have more endurance.
